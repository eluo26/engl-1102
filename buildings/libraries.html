Building History

The Libraries: Price Gilbert, Crosland & CULC

From Price Gilbert (1953) to Crosland Tower (1969) to the Clough Commons, tracing how the library grew with campus.

Price Gilbert renovation photo by JJonahJackalope (2019), CC BY-SA 4.0; cropped and darkened for the hero.

01

Crossland Tower

Origins

As Georgia Tech grew, the need for space for graduate students, research, and library materials grew. The Price Gilbert Library opened in 1953 but was overcrowded by the 1960s. Dorothy Crosland, the library director, had long advocated a new addition primarily for graduate students and the school’s expanding research activities. Construction started in 1966, and the Graduate Addition was dedicated in 1969. This growth allowed Georgia Tech to offer more resources as the university expanded (Ray & Ellis Consulting 15-16).

Timeline of Expansion

1964 - Planning Begins
Georgia Tech begins planning a new graduate library addition because the existing building is running out of space (Ray & Ellis Consulting 15).

1966 - Construction Begins
The groundbreaking ceremony takes place on October 20, officially beginning the expansion (Ray & Ellis Consulting 16).

1969- GRaduate Addition Opens
The nine story addition is dedicated on November 21, providing additional study and research space (Li et al. 4)

1985 - Renamed Crosland Tower
The building is renamed in honor of Dorothy Crosland for her contributions to Georgia Tech’s library system (Li et al. 4).

Renovations and Who is Served

Crosland Tower was built to provide graduate students with additional space to study and conduct research. The original building contained seating, study rooms, and space for a growing collection of books. Georgia Tech started to change the use of its library buildings over the years. Much of the physical collection had been relocated to off-site storage to accommodate the renovation of the older buildings, as outlined in the Library’s 2019–2022 Strategic Plan. This allowed for more flexible spaces for students, technology, and collaboration (Li et al. 4; Georgia Tech Library, Strategic Plan).

Student Voices

According to historical records, the Graduate Addition was constructed to give students more study space. When it opened, the building had about 2,000 seats, individual study areas, seminar rooms, and other resources for graduate students (Li et al. 4).

This illustrates how Crosland Tower enabled Georgia Tech to respond to the needs of its growing academic community.

Legacy

Crosland Tower is a significant step in the evolution of Georgia Tech as a research institution. The building was designed to house more graduate students and research materials, but it was also part of an overall plan to modernize the campus library.

Georgia Tech’s history is a reflection of the continuous evolution and modification of academic spaces to suit the evolving needs of their students (Ray & Ellis Consulting 15-16; Georgia Tech Library, Strategic Plan).

02

Clough Undergraduate Learning Commons

Origin -

Georgia Tech’s student population continues to grow, and the school needed more classrooms, laboratories and places for students to work together. In 1999, Georgia Tech president G. Wayne Clough announced the plans for a new Undergraduate Learning Center. At the time, Georgia Tech’s enrollment had grown from about 8,000 students to 14,000 students in three decades. The proposed building will bring together academic resources, technology and study areas in one central location. This project was an important part of Georgia Tech’s growth because it focused on increasing space for undergraduate students and improving their learning experience (Clough 1-4).

Timeline of Expansion -

1997 - Technology Requirements
Georgia Tech requires entering undergraduate students to have computer systems that connect to its campus network (Clough 5).

1999 - Undergraduate Learning Center Proposed
G. Wayne Clough presents plans for a 225,000 square foot facility intended to address Georgia Tech’s expanded academic needs (Clough 1).

1999 - Learning Center Design
Plans emphasize technology equipped classrooms, group meeting spaces, library resources, and academic support service (Clough 6-8).

2019-2022 - Connected Library Development
Georgia Tech’s strategic plan describes how Clough, Price Gilbert, and Crosland would work together as connected learning environments (Georgia Tech Library, Strategic Plan 9).

Then vs. Now

Renovation and Who It Served

Clough Commons was designed to address several problems that Georgia Tech was facing as its enrollment increased. Many of the school’s older classrooms and labs were built before modern technology became an important part of education. The proposed Undergraduate Learning Center would give students newer classrooms, study areas and academic support services. The building also would clear space in the existing library so it could one day be renovated, Clough said. This shows how Georgia Tech’s growth was linked to improving new and existing campus buildings (Clough 3–8).

Student Voices -

In 1999, G. Wayne Clough said in a presentation that students were already comfortable with technology and expected it to be part of their education. He explained the reason for having a more interactive learning environment where students could connect with one another and have technology in their hands throughout their education (Clough 4).

This means Georgia Tech was trying to grow in a way that would better match how students were learning.

Legacy -

Clough Commons is a key piece of Georgia Tech’s growth in that it focused on putting more undergraduate resources in one place. Originally the plans called for modern classrooms, new technology and places for students to collaborate. The building was

also integrated into the larger library system, as were Price Gilbert and Crosland Tower. This is representative of how Georgia Tech was expanding its campus to accommodate its growing student population and new forms of education (Clough 6-9; Georgia Tech Library, Strategic Plan 9).

03

Price Gilbert Memorial Library

Origin -

The original Carnegie Library was too small for the number of students and books on campus as Georgia Tech grew after World War II. Dorothy Crosland realized the need for a larger library and began to campaign for a new building. Construction began in 1951, and the Price Gilbert Memorial Library was officially dedicated on November 21, 1953. The building was named for Judge S. Price Gilbert, who financially supported the project. The library held about 800 students and gave much more room for study and research. This was a major element to Georgia Tech’s expansion as the campus needed more academic resources to support its growing population (Li et al. 2-3).

Timeline of Expansion -

1951 - Construction Begins
Georgia Tech breaks ground on July 5 for the new library (Ray & Ellis Consulting 14-15).

1953 - Library Opens
Price Gilbert opens in November, providing more room for students, staff, and library materials (Li et al. 2-3).

1969 - Graduate Addition Opens
A second library building is added to provide more space for graduate research (Li et al. 4).

2020 - Library Transformation
Georgia Tech’s strategic plan identifies the reopening of the renovated Price Gilbert Library as the final major stage of the Library Next project (Georgia Tech Library, Strategic Plan).

Then vs Now

What the Price Library Looks like Nowadays.

Renovation and Who It Served -

As Georgia Tech grew and technology became more important to education, the Price Gilbert Library had to change over time. The library was initially intended to store books and provide a space to study. With the digitization of more academic resources, Georgia Tech started moving much of its physical collection off campus. This allowed for the complete renovation of Price Gilbert, creating spaces for collaboration, multimedia work, teaching and research. The changes also assisted in connecting Price Gilbert with Crosland Tower and Clough Commons, thus providing more connectivity in the learning environment for students (Georgia Tech Library, Strategic Plan 9).

From the Archives -

Student Voices -

The Price Gilbert Library quickly became a part of student life when it opened. During the academic year 1956–1957, 2,700 students attended classes in the Music Room, 5,425 people registered to use the listening rooms, and 7,105 students participated in library instruction classes. These figures demonstrate that students were utilizing Price Gilbert for much more than checking out books (Li et al. 3).

Legacy -

The Price Gilbert Library is a symbol of Georgia Tech’s ongoing growth and change on its campus. When the building first opened, it solved the problem of overcrowding in the old library. Decades later, its renovation was to create new space for technology, research and collaboration. The Price Gilbert building is an example of Georgia Tech’s continued expansion of its academic buildings to meet the needs of students as the university grows (Li et al. 2-3; Georgia Tech Library, Strategic Plan 9).
