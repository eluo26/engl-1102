The Project

Georgia Tech opened with two buildings and now has over two hundred. Expansion has never really been in question; it has defined the campus for its entire history. This project asks a different question: how have the physical spaces of campus shaped the people who learned, ate, studied, and lived in them? We follow significant buildings through construction, renovation, and restoration, and ask who each change served.

Who This Is For

We wrote this for the Georgia Tech community: current and prospective students, alumni, faculty, and neighbors in the surrounding area of Atlanta.

What does this audience already know, and how did that shape our choices?

For example: most readers know these buildings as they look today but not their history. That is why the story opens on a current campus map and every building page includes a “Then & Now” comparison. Alumni may remember earlier versions; prospective students may not know the buildings at all.

Our Position

As Georgia Tech students, we generally see expansion as good for campus and the city. Others in our audience, including neighbors affected by campus growth, may disagree. We aim to present the history objectively, relying on sources that document development rather than argue for or against it.

What does each of us bring to this story? Majors, research, family ties to Tech, first-generation experience, personal connections to a building.

Why It Matters

There is little comprehensive, public-facing history of how Georgia Tech's campus developed. Many of the archival maps and images we use are hard to find online. This site gathers them in one place as a small, accessible archive.

What does this add to the stories already told about Tech? Who made the decisions to build, and who was affected by them?

Expand on the stakes: whose needs drove each expansion, who was served or displaced, and what this record makes possible for readers today.
